# Disconnecting Cables
If it hasn't been done already, power down everything on the trailer except the lights and breaker 2 (which charges the hydraulics battery). The lights can stay on until the hydraulic walls come up.

When powering down, make sure breakers 5 and 6 are flipped down (green), then disconnect the two multi-plugs on the floor on both sides of the trailer — the ones that attach to the 16 amp single-phase cables. None of the frames should have power after this.

![Disconnect 16 amp cable](images/16_amp_disconect.webp)

1. Disconnect the 2 × Sony ESPA power boxes. Take all the DC power cables (the ones with blue tape, connecting the Sonys to the ESPA boxes) and store them as a group in the large plastic tub — it will probably be the one holding the FACS Sony lenses.

![Stowed Sony power cables](images/sony_cables_stored.webp)

2. Disconnect the kettle lead from each ESPA power brick and wrap it neatly around a nearby light.

![wrap around light](images/sony_kettle.webp)

3. Store both ESPA boxes and their power bricks in the plastic tub.

![sony power stowed](images/espas_stored.webp)

4. Unplug everything attached to the now-disconnected multi-plugs. Wrap each plug and its cable (the ones that feed the frames' multi-plugs) around a nearby light, to keep them stowed neatly off the ground.

![Disconnect power](images/multi-plug_disconect.webp)

5. Take the floor multi-plugs and stow them in the plastic tub.

![stow multi-plugs](images/multi-plug_disconect.webp)

6. Find the Canon ESPA box on Frame 2. Disconnect all its DC power inputs and tuck them up into the frame so they don't drag on the ground.

7. Disconnect its kettle lead from its power brick. This one is too short to reach a light, so just leave it hanging where it is.

8. Store that ESPA box and its power brick in the plastic tub.

9. Find the trigger cable that runs under the door mat to connect both sides (frame 12 to frame 9). Disconnect it at one end and wrap it around a nearby light.

![wrap around light](images/door_cablewebp.webp)

10. Find the trigger cable that runs from frame 9 to the LED Arduino. Detach it from the frame and wrap it around the power cables by the Arduino.

![cable running from ardunio to Frame 9](images/led_cable.webp)

![led cables stowed](images/led_cables_stowed.webp)

By this point all the cables from the rig should be off the floor, so this is the time to do a visual inspection of the rig. If you see any cables still touching the floor, try to attach them back onto the rig. There are zip ties in the trailer if you need them.

# Collapsing the Rig
Take the marker board out and put it somewhere out of the way — you will need it again later. Take the colour chart and stow it in the open top drawer of the PC cage.

Before collapsing the rig, find the metal bolts that slot into the spring-loaded holes in the floor. If you are standing in the trailer door facing the rig, there are two on the left side and three on the right. Pull each bolt up and lock it in the higher position.

![un-bolted](images/floor_bolt_up.webp)

::: important
There is one more bolt closest to the main PC, between frame 1 and frame 20. Leave that one attached — it holds the middle of the rig in place while you fold the sides around it.
:::

Walk around the rig and, for all the cameras on the bottom of the frames (the Canon zoom lenses), fold their screens into the camera so they don't stick out.

![cameras screen flipped in](images/camera_screen_collapsed.webp)

Then walk around and release the brakes on all the frames by flicking the black plastic tab by the wheels so it is horizontal.

## Folding the right side
Start with the right side. You are going to slowly curl the rig so that it becomes a thin "U" shape, which means moving frame 11 towards the back of the trailer.

![right side, fully folded](images/right_side_closed.webp)

Go slowly — move around and pull different frames so they all travel at a similar rate. You will meet resistance; if you do, check whether a wire has fallen under a wheel. If not, it's likely a wheel is up against one of the long bumps on the floor. The easiest way to move over the bumps is to change sides and push the frame with a foot, applying pressure to the metal bar with the wheels.

Frame 11 should end up at the back of the trailer, wedged under the black bar running across the roof near the back. There is an obvious spot for it — you can see it in the scratches on the paintwork of the top bar.

![frame 11 under the bar](images/frame_11_under_bar.webp)

Make sure the frames are compressed properly. Running down the length of the trailer floor there are patches of purple tape; use these as your marker. All the frame wheels should sit inside the tape, towards the centre of the trailer.

![frame wheels inside the purple tape](images/both_sides_closed.webp)

Once this side has collapsed, walk around and apply the brake to every wheel on it.

Find two of the metal cables with green pins — usually stored in a corner of the trailer. Head to frame 11 and find the two eyelets on the side of the frame closest to the back of the trailer. On the horizontal bar above there are another two eyelets. Connect each eyelet on frame 11 to the closest eyelet on the horizontal bar.

![frame 11 connected](images/frame_11_cable.webp)

::: note
If the eyelets are too slack, you may be able to twist them and slide them along the metal — though some of them won't move.
:::

Take 2 × ratchet straps and wrap each one around both the inner frame and the outer frame to bind them together. Put them around the middle of the frames, and catch **only the vertical bars** — taking in the horizontal bars as well pulls the frames out of position. Do them up firmly, but not so tight that they bend the frames. The point of these extra straps is to stop the middle frames moving so much in transit.

![middle ratchet](images/middle_ratchet.webp)

![not too tight](images/midde_ratchet_tension.webp)

## Folding the left side
Now collapse the left side of the trailer. Again, go slowly, pulling and pushing different sections to spread the force. This side curls around the middle frames: frame 12 will end up wrapped all the way around the middle and over to the right side of the trailer, like an inverted "J".

![both sides folded](images/left_side_wrapped_on_right_side.webp)

Once again, make sure all the frames are inside the purple tape, then go around and apply the brakes to every wheel.

Take another 2 × ratchet straps and wrap them around the middle of the outer and inner frames to bind them together, exactly as you did on the other side — vertical bars only. Again, make sure they aren't so tight that they bend the frames in towards each other.

## Cabling the rig to the ceiling
Find 4 × white cables with green pins and use two on each side. Look for the eyelets in the grey aluminium bars running along the trailer's ceiling from back to front; there are corresponding eyelets on the top of the outer frames. Connect two on each side, preferably near the middle — the corner ratchet straps hold the corners, and they go on at the very end of the packdown.

![cable attached](images/middle_frame_cable.webp)

Some of the eyelets can be moved along the metal by unscrewing them slightly and sliding them, but again, not all of them will.

# Tidying Everything Away
Now the rig is closed, it's time to tidy and store all the non-rig items away.

1. Head to the PC tower and disconnect all the cables from the monitor. Lay the monitor flat, face down on top of the tower, and tape the screen down so it can't move in transit.

![monitor ready for transpor](images/scnreen_stowed.webp)

2. Put the mouse and keyboard inside the cage, then shut the cage door and twist it so it locks.

3. If you used them, collapse the chairs and table. They should stow behind the PC tower against the back wall.

![chair and table stowed](images/chairs_stowed.webp)

4. Stack all the tubs, tools and boxes in front of the PC cage. There's no definitive order — just get them tucked in nicely, with little space around them to rattle.


5. Add the last few items, like the broom, into the gaps.

6. Place the marker board sideways at the front of the stored objects, so it sits between the front of the PC cage and the rig. It acts as a dam, stopping anything spilling out into the rig.


![items stowed](images/items_stowed.webp)

7. Take the two grey dehumidifiers off the walls and clip one to the middle of the outside of the rig on each side.

8. Stack all the foam either outside or by the stairs — somewhere accessible that isn't on either side of the trailer, as you will need it once the first side panels are down.

9. Do a final inspection of the floor: everything should have a place, and the side floors should be clear.

![floor clear](images/side_clear.webp)

::: note
The 2 × 16 amp cables will still be running across the floor at this point — that's expected. They get pulled back through and stowed in the next section.
:::

## Strapping the rig down
Now that everything is stowed and nothing is left to move around the rig, put the corner straps on. Leaving them until last means you aren't working over them while you tidy.

Take 4 × ratchet straps and, in each corner of the rig, find the metal loop in the floor — these are the mount points for the straps. Thread a strap through each loop and wrap it around the top corner of the rig, trying to get it around at least two frames to bind them. These can be done up very tight.

![wrap the strap around the frame](images/ratchet_wrapping.webp)

![racthed attached](images/corner_ratchet.webp)

# Storing Cables and Power — Part One
Head outside to the ends of your 2 × 16 amp cables. If they aren't already unplugged, remove them from the distro box or power source (or from the extensions, if you are using them).

Head back inside and start pulling them through the hole in the floor on the left side of the trailer. With two people, one can stay outside feeding the cable through and keeping the knots out of it. On your own, you may need to alternate between inside and outside when it snags or tangles.

Pull the two cables through until their ports hang directly under the hole with no extra cable — the ports will not come inside the trailer.

Take all the slack you've pulled up into the trailer and store it behind the marker board, or at least in that general area, where the bulk of it is clear of the collapsing sides.

![slack stowed](images/16_amp_cables_stowed.webp)

::: note
Leave the 32 amp power plugged in for now — it's your interior lighting for the rest of the packdown.
:::

# Collapsing the Sides — Part One
Now you should be ready to collapse the sides. Head inside and find the two pieces of rectangular white foam. Place them on the floor where the lights (the ones with the Lidar Lounge logo) will touch when the wall comes down.


![foam that protects the side lights](images/foam_for_light.webp)

::: note
The 2 × 16 amp single-phase cables will still be running along the floor, and that's fine — as long as there's no real slack on the floor, the wall will close over them.
:::

Head to a wall and find the four connection points that lock it in place. At the top corner on either side there is a spring-assisted locking bolt and a metal locking hinge. Pull the bolt back and lock it so it's free of the metal; for the hinge, push the slider on the front up to unlock it, then position it so the loop is free of the ceiling.

![wall connecitons to undo](images/side_connectors_open.webp)

![hinge open](images/hinge_openwebp.webp)

With nothing locking it, grab the handle in the middle of the wall and pull it towards you, lowering the wall down.

Once it's down, check that nothing is caught and that the wall is flat.

::: important
It's extremely common for one or both of the metal loops on the hinges to dangle down vertically as the wall collapses, and end up propping the wall up. You will spot it because the wall won't sit flat in the corners. To fix it, hold the wall up slightly and give the dangling loop a nudge with a foot to keep it from catching as you lower the wall back down.
:::

![hinge propping up wall](images/hinge_caught.webp)

Repeat all of these steps on the opposite side of the trailer.

With both sides down, head to the breaker box and — if you haven't already — flip the main breaker at the top down (showing green) to shut off the 32 amp power to the trailer.

Now find the foam you moved earlier. The large pieces with cutouts in them sit over the back of some of the light pairs. The rest of the foam can be laid on the floor by the edges of the frames.

# Storing Cables and Power — Part Two
Head outside to your 32 amp cable and, if you haven't already, disconnect it from the distro box, power source or extension. Bring the cable round to the back of the trailer and start pulling it through the small hole into the cupboard with the hydraulics and car battery. When you get to the port you will have to twist it and apply some pressure until it pops through.

If you used any extensions, disconnect them from the distro or power source, coil them up and store them in the back of the trailer on the shelf above the cupboard with the hydraulics and car battery.

![distro stowed](images/distro_store.webp)

If you used our distro box and/or our 63 amp cable, disconnect them from the power source — the 63 amp cable has to be twisted before it will come out. Store the distro box and the 63 amp cable on the shelf.

# Collapsing the Sides — Part Two
You will need to switch the hydraulics on. Open the shutter door at the back by the hitch point. On the left side, to the right of the AC unit, you should find a red turn-switch — twist it so it is vertical to enable the hydraulics.

In the app, go to the Extras page. There will be Extra 1 and Extra 2 — one for each side. A trailer diagram will confirm which side you are controlling; the dot in the diagram is the hitch point, so use that to orient yourself.

To raise the sides, press and hold RETRACT and the side should start rising. Around two times out of ten — less often than when extending — the hydraulics battery will fail, the app will lose connection and the side won't move. If this happens at any stage of raising the sides, you will have to finish with the manual override system.

## Manual override
In the rear of the trailer, behind the shutter door on the right, you will find the hydraulic override.

There are two silver metal rods with twisty tops; these control what operation the manual crank will perform. The rod in front controls the right side, and the rod at the back controls the left side. Each rod has three states: raise, neutral, and lower.

On the back of the rod you will see a small pin and some channels cut into the rod for the pin to slide through. It looks and behaves similarly to a manual transmission in a car, but instead of 5–6 gears there are three. Each position puts the hydraulics into one of the three states:

- **Top:** raise

- **Middle:** neutral

- **Bottom:** lower

To move the pin, twist the top of the rod to move it laterally, and pull or push to move it vertically.

To continue, put one pin into the top position and make sure the other is in neutral. Then retrieve the crank bar from its place on the inside of the cabinet door next to you. To the right of the control rods you will see a hole that the crank bar slots into.

1.  Crank the bar from left to right. It should feel relatively easy; if it is resisting hard, stop and double-check the pins, as you may be trying to lower the side.

2.  After about ten cranks, go and visually check that the desired side is rising. It takes around five minutes of cranking to fully raise a side.

3.  Keep cranking until you feel resistance.

## Locking the raised side
Once the side is completely raised, find the two spring bolts on the sides of the panel. Release each bolt and slide it into the side to lock the panel in place. The panel should have some movement in it, so you may need to give it a shove before the bolt will slide in. In rare cases the bolt may be jammed because there is too much pressure on it; if this happens, raise the legs on this side of the trailer and/or lower the legs on the opposite side, so the weight of the trailer leans away from the panel.

![side bolted](images/side_bolt_locked.webp)

Repeat these steps in exactly the same way on the opposite side of the trailer.

You can now switch the hydraulics off (turn the red switch sideways).

## Folding in the panels and the roof
Once both sides are raised, find the four backup metal legs. Now that the full weight is off them they should be fairly easy to undo. Remove all four and stow them on the floor at the front of the trailer.

On each side, fold the side panels in until they rest against the wall. Once they are tucked in, you can bring the roof down.

Bringing the roof down is very physically demanding and often needs two people. There is a handle made from the red cable and small carabiners in the middle, which helps if the roof is out of reach. Pull it down hard, then push until it folds into the side of the trailer.

On the side of the trailer there are three connections to lock. At either end is a spring-assisted locking bolt: pull it down, twist it until it's released, then let it snap up into place. In the centre is a red cable with two small carabiners — detach one carabiner and clip it to the small hole in the frame of the trailer's chassis.

::: note
If the trailer is heading out for a multi-day transit, or nobody will be there on the other side to receive it, you may want to lock the front of the trailer and give the keys to the driver. The keys are in a clear plastic ziplock bag inside a small white panel, found at the top of the stairs on the left (if you are facing the trailer), near the door.
:::

## The stairs
Head over to the stairs, stand at the top and pull the roof over the stairs down.

::: important
Be careful — water pools on top of this section when it rains, and it's easy to soak yourself.
:::

Find the two spring-loaded bolts on the corners of the now-collapsed roof section, then twist and release them so they slide into the sides, locking the roof in place.

Head down the stairs, lift them up and give them a hard push so they lock into place and become the front door. If you are locking this door with the keys, this is the time.

Finally, find a small carabiner and attach it to the stair section — it's an emergency backup in case the stairs try to open in transit.

![door cable with small carabiners](images/door_crab.webp)

# Loading the Trailer
The trailer is now ready to be moved. The hydraulic feet should stay down, supporting the weight, until a vehicle arrives to collect it.

When the driver arrives, turn the hydraulics on with the small red switch, flipping it vertical.

Head to the back by the hitch point and wind up the jockey wheel. Once it's high enough it should pivot 90 degrees, and a lever will lock it in position out of the way.

## Onto a flatbed recovery lorry
If the trailer is heading straight onto a flat recovery lorry, use the HPC app to RETRACT all the legs so it is sitting only on its wheels. If you are able, unlock the handbrake by pulling the black lever by the hitch point; if you can't, or you're unsure, ask the driver, as they will be familiar with it.

Sometimes the trailer has trouble getting onto a flatbed, thanks to terrible design. This is not your job to solve — let the driver figure it out. You should tell them that you have control over the legs, though, as they may ask you to raise or lower them at several points to help.

Once it's on the flatbed and the driver is happy, EXTEND the legs using the HPC app until they are once again taking the weight of the trailer. Don't worry about getting the trailer level — it will probably sit at a slight angle on the flatbed. Instead, get the trailer's floor as parallel to the bed's floor as you can.

Once it's stable, hop up onto the flatbed and switch the hydraulics off by flipping the red switch horizontal.

## Onto a tow bar
If it's being picked up by a van or car and going on a tow bar, explain to the driver that you can lift the hitch point of the trailer using the hydraulic legs. Raise the hitch point, let the driver reverse until their ball joint is directly under the hitch, then lower the hitch onto the joint.

Once it's on, RETRACT all the legs through the HPC app if you haven't already.

If you have locked the trailer, give the driver the ziplock bag of keys, so they stay with the trailer.

Finally, switch the hydraulics off by flipping the red switch horizontal.
