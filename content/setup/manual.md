# Unloading the Trailer
The trailer will usually arrive in one of two ways: either on a flatbed-style recovery truck / transport lorry, or towed behind a van.

In both cases you will need to switch on the hydraulics. To do this, open the shutter door at the back by the hitch point. On the left side, to the right of the AC unit, you should find a red turn-switch — twist it so it is vertical to enable the hydraulics.

![Red turn-switch, vertical = hydraulics on](images/hydrolics_off.webp)

Once the hydraulics are on, the trailer should appear in the HPC hydraulic app so you can control it. If it does not appear after a few minutes, the car battery powering it may be dead. You should find a jump kit in the back section.

## Using the jump pack
Connect the jump pack in this order:

1.  **Red to Dead (+):** attach the red positive clamp to the positive (+) terminal on the dead battery.

<!-- -->

1.  **Black to Ground (–):** attach the black negative clamp to a solid, unpainted metal part away from the battery.

When finished, detach in reverse order.

If the jump pack is also dead, you will need a jump from a car or van. You should find jump cables in the back.

## Using jump cables from another vehicle
When attaching jump cables, do it in this order:

1.  **Red to Dead (+):** connect the first red clamp to the positive (+) terminal of the dead battery.

<!-- -->

1.  **Red to Live (+):** connect the other red clamp to the positive (+) terminal of the live battery.

2.  **Black to Live (–):** connect the first black clamp to the negative (–) terminal of the live battery.

3.  **Black to Dead Ground (–):** connect the final black clamp to an unpainted metal part away from the dead battery.

When finished, detach in reverse order.

If the trailer arrives on the recovery truck, its legs will have been extended to support it during the journey, so once the driver says it is safe, retract the legs fully. It is also worth checking that the driver has unlocked the handbrake lever by the hitch point, as many forget this.

# Choosing a Position
Production will have chosen a rough spot for the trailer to be set up, but you are in charge of picking the exact position. Use the following criteria:

- Extra space on the sides so it can expand during setup — at least 2 metres.

<!-- -->

- The ground is not too uneven. The hydraulic legs stabilise very well on unstable ground, but there is a limit to how much they can do.

- There is space for the stairs — at least 1.2 metres.

- The ground at the bottom of the stairs is not much higher than the ground under the trailer, otherwise the trailer door cannot be opened.

If the trailer is being towed and is in position, you may need to help the driver's vehicle detach from the hitch. Once the hitch is no longer locked, you can use the legs to raise the trailer, lift the hitch off the driver's tow bar, and they can then drive forward.

Once the trailer is in position and detached, you can stabilise the legs. There is a button for this in the app, and you can also use manual mode with the app's spirit level if you need more control.

Lower the hitch wheel by rotating the lever until it starts to take some of the weight, then make sure the handbrake by the hitch is on. As much as possible, we want the weight held by the legs rather than the tyres.

::: important
Please check that the stairs can be lowered properly and the trailer door can be opened before the driver leaves. If you can't get in, the driver will have to help reposition the trailer.
:::

# Asking for Power
Once the trailer is in position, the next thing to do is check whether power is available. You won't need it for the next part of the setup, but it can take a while for production to sort out power, so the sooner it's arranged the better.

The trailer has three input ports for power, and it needs all three:

- **1 × 32 amp 3-phase** (large cable with a red port)

- **2 × 16 amp single-phase** (smaller cables with a blue port)

Mostly you will get power from a nearby generator, but sometimes it's studio power. Unfortunately we have unusual power requirements and will usually need a distribution box (distro) to convert the generator's output to the correct outputs for the trailer.


Luckily, we have our own distro box that can take an input of 1 × 63 amp 3-phase (larger cable with a red port) and output the correct power for the trailer.

![Our own distro](images/distro.webp)


You will get used to asking for: “1 × 32 amp 3-phase and 2 × 16 amp single-phase, or just 1 × 63 amp 3-phase.”

Most generators will have a 63 amp 3-phase output, so inspect for that. If there are already distro boxes on the generator, check whether there are enough of the correct outputs for us.

If there is not the correct power — or usually no power at all — message your production contact and let them know, as a lack of power will grind the setup to a halt.

If you do get confused by the power, try to get in contact with the sparks on set and they will figure it out pretty quickly.

# Opening the Sides
On the side of the trailer there are three connections to undo before it can be lifted. On either end is a spring-assisted locking bolt — pull the bolt down and twist it so it is caught and can't snap back.

![bolt closed](images/spring_bolt_under_side.webp)

![bolt open](images/spring_bolt_under_side_open.webp)

In the centre is a red cable with two small carabiners. One carabiner will be around a hole in the frame of the trailer's chassis; unclip it and attach it to where the other carabiner is. This also creates a useful handle for packdown.

![red cable](images/red_handle.webp)

![un clip](images/red_carabiner_attach_point.webp)

![re clip](images/red_cable_as_handle.webp)

Once the side has all three connections undone, you should be able to grab the bottom and lift it up. You won't be able to lift it fully, but enough for it to be held by its own tension.


![roof open](images/roof_up_side.webp)

This should reveal two white panels on the trailer's side. They are only held in by a weak magnet at the centre, so they should pull away from the middle easily, pivoting on the edges. As these panels come out they will hit the side you lifted earlier and push it up. Slowly alternate between each panel as you pull them out, and they will lift the side to its full height as it becomes the ceiling of the expanded section.

![pulling out expanding sides](images/opening_side_panel.webp)

![fully open](images/side_panel_open.webp)

Now there will be a single large white panel left on the side of the trailer. Each side has a single connection point — another spring-assisted locking bolt. 

![un-bolt](images/white_panel_bolt_open.webp)

Unbolt each side. The panel should have some movement in it, so you may need to give it a shove so the bolt can come free. In rare cases the bolt may be jammed because there is too much pressure on it; if this happens, raise the legs on this side of the trailer and/or lower the legs on the opposite side so the weight of the trailer leans away from the panel.



::: important
Repeat these steps in exactly the same way on the opposite side of the trailer. This is vital, because you don't want to accidentally try to lower a side that is still connected.
:::

![ready for hydaulics](images/sides_wall_open.webp)

# Lowering the Hydraulic Sides
Once both hydraulic side panels have been exposed and unbolted, it's time to lower them. Make sure the hydraulics are on (red switch twisted vertical).

In the app, go to the Extras page. There will be Extra 1 and Extra 2 — one for each side. A trailer diagram will confirm which side you are controlling; the dot in the diagram is the hitch point, so use that to orient yourself.

To lower the sides, press and hold EXTEND and the side should start lowering. However, around seven times out of ten the hydraulics battery will fail, the app will lose connection, and the side won't move. If this happens at any stage of lowering the sides, you will have to finish with the manual override system.

## Manual override
In the rear of the trailer, behind the shutter door on the right, you will find the hydraulic override.

There are two silver metal rods with twisty tops; these control what operation the manual crank will perform. The rod in front controls the right side, and the rod at the back controls the left side. Each rod has three states: raise, neutral, and lower.

![right = front, left = back](images/manual_override_pilons.webp)

On the back of the rod you will see a small pin and some channels cut into the rod for the pin to slide through. It looks and behaves similarly to a manual transmission in a car, but instead of 5–6 gears there are three. Each position puts the hydraulics into one of the three states:

- **Top:** raise

- **Middle:** neutral

- **Bottom:** lower

To move the pin, twist the top of the rod to move it laterally, and pull or push to move it vertically.

![up,neutrual,down](images/manual_overide.webp)

To continue, put one pin into the bottom position and make sure the other is in neutral. Then retrieve the crank bar from its place on the inside of the cabinet door next to you. To the right of the control rods you will see a hole that the crank bar slots into.

1.  Crank the bar from left to right. It should feel relatively easy; if it is resisting hard, stop and double-check the pins, as you may be trying to raise the side.

<!-- -->

1.  After about ten cranks, go and visually check that the desired side is lowering. It takes around five minutes of cranking to fully lower a side.

2.  The side being lowered has two metal pins at its corners, designed to slot into openings on the expanded sides. If there are two of you, one person should stay by the side and may have to pull the expanded sides closer into the lowering side so the pins line up.

![pin and hole](images/side_lowering.webp)

3.  Keep cranking until you feel resistance, then check the pins. They often catch on the lip of the opening; if they do, give the expanded side a shove and the pin should fall into the hole.

![pin caught](images/side_pin_alignment.webp)

4.  Make sure both pins on the side are slotted into the expanded-wall holes, and give a final crank check to make sure it's fully down. Don't try to push past resistance.

![side lowered](images/side_lowered.webp)

## Fitting the support legs
Head inside to grab the four metal legs. Go to a corner of the trailer and place one underneath so that it touches both the expanded side and the lowered floor panel, then twist it so it wedges itself between the ground and the corner.

![leg](images/support_leg.webp)

![leg in postion](images/support_leg_attached.webp)

::: note
A trick with these metal legs: get them to almost the right height before placing them underneath. To change the height quickly, hold the leg in one hand to stop the nut from spinning, and with your free hand swivel the foot around to change its height.
:::

Repeat this on the other three corners.

## Raising the final side
Now it's time to raise the final side. Head inside the trailer and stand in the middle, on the edge of the rig. Bend down and lift the side up — it is fairly heavy, so you may need help.

Once it's raised there are four connection points to lock it in place. On either side, at the top corner, there is a spring-assisted locking bolt and a metal locking hinge.

![bolt and hinge](images/wall_bolts.webp)

Release the bolt so it slides behind the metal. For the hinge, push the slider on the front up to unlock it, position it so the loop is caught by the ceiling, then pull it down so it locks. Make sure all four connections are attached, then repeat on the other side.

# Powering the Trailer
By this point you may have noticed it's very dark in the trailer — the next thing you need is power. If there is still no sign of power, chase up your production coordinator and explain that you are now unable to continue until power arrives.

::: note
All the cable types are different, so don't worry about connecting something wrong — only the correct ports will connect.
:::

## 1 × 32 amp 3-phase (large cable with a red port)
This input cable is found in the rear of the trailer behind the shutter door, resting on top of the battery. Take the end and try to feed it through the small hole at the back right corner of the space it's in. It won't fully fit, so go around and try pulling from the other side; give it a few twists and it should reluctantly pop through.

![almost out](images/32_amp_port.webp)

Run the cable to the power source (distro box or generator). If you don't have enough cable, there is a large extension inside the back of the trailer, in the section above the battery and hydraulics.

## 2 × 16 amp single-phase (smaller cables with a blue port)
These cables are coiled inside the main area of the trailer, with their ports hanging outside the bottom. You will need to feed the cable through the hole — it's easier with two people, one inside and one outside, but it's perfectly doable by yourself.

Once enough cable has been fed through to the outside, connect them to a power source (distro or generator). If the cables don't reach, extensions can be found in the rear of the trailer, in the section above the battery and hydraulics.

::: note
If you are using a distribution box (some setups may have more than one in the system), check that the breaker switches for the ports you need are flipped up so that red is showing. Red means on (danger); green means off (safe).
:::

## Switching on at the breaker box
Head back inside the trailer and go to the back to find the main breaker box. Flip the large breaker at the top to enable the 32 amp power to the trailer, then — if they aren't up already — flip breaker 2 (charges the hydraulics battery) and breaker 4 (labelled LIGHTS).

Now the ceiling lights should be on and you can start expanding the rig.

# Expanding the Rig

::: important
First, inspect the rig for any damage. If there is any, please let David or Theo know and we can advise.
:::

The rig will have been secured with ratchet straps and metal cables. There are four ratchet straps, one in each corner of the rig — undo them all and store them neatly in a corner.

![corner ratchet straps](images/corner_ratchet_flocked.webp)

Next, find the metal cables that attach the rig to the ceiling bar. Unscrew the green pins to remove them, and try to keep the pins attached to the cable ends when storing.

![try not to drop the pin](images/green_pin.webp)

There will be a cable with a carabiner wrapped around the door frame on the right side near the trailer entrance — remove that as well.

There will be two more metal cables attached to the central frame near the rear of the trailer. These won't be accessible until you have unfolded one half.

## Clearing the clutter
Now is the best time to clear the clutter. Take all the foam used for packing and put it in a corner of the trailer.

Take the long grey dehumidifiers from the sides of the rig and place them on the handles at the top of the trailer's inside walls.

Take everything stored near the main PC and put it safely in the trailer's corners, or at least out of the way of where the rig will need to expand into.

Make a final check that nothing is on the floor where the rig will end up — this includes the 16 amp single-phase cables that run outside.

## Unfurling the rig
Now it's time to start unfurling. If you stand in the door facing the rig, you will start with the side to your left — the side the 16 amp cables exit through.

1.  Walk along that side of the rig and release the brakes on all the frames by flicking the black plastic tab by the wheels so it's horizontal.

<!-- -->

1.  There should not be any cables dragging on the ground. If there are, carefully try to re-attach them to the frame.

2.  Carefully begin pulling the frames apart. It's best to go slowly — move around and pull different frames so they all move at a similar rate. You will meet resistance; if you do, check whether a wire has fallen under a wheel. If not, it's likely a wheel is up against one of the long bumps on the floor. The easiest way to move over the bumps is to change sides and push the frame with a foot, applying pressure to the metal bar with the wheels.

3.  Get the frames into a rough semi-circle shape, then look at the floor. You will find two metal circles — these are spring-loaded holes. There are two metal locking bolts on the bottom of the frames, which should be near the holes; find them, then pull and wiggle the frame until the bolt is over the hole and slide it in to fix the frame in position.

![left side open](images/left_side_unfurled.webp)

4.  Now that the left side is open, you have access to the last cables tying the middle frame to the ceiling bar. Unscrew the green pins and store the cables.

5.  Begin unfurling the other side in a similar, careful fashion. On this side there are three bolts, not two. The door frame should end up near the trailer door and line up with the other side of the frame (there will be a gap between the two sides).

6.  Find the marker board and place it in the middle of the rig — there should be black and red tape markers for where it's positioned.

![fully open](images/full_rig_oppen.webp)

7.  Stand in the middle of the marker board and look at each frame in turn; they should all face you. If they don't, adjust them. Once you're done you should have a cohesive circle where every frame points forward, to the centre.

8.  Go around and turn on the frame brakes by flipping the black plastic tab by the wheel so it points up.

# Powering the Rig
Now the rig is expanded, it's time to turn all the cameras on. Find one of the bigger plastic tubs that was stored next to the main PC and transit; inside you will find 2 × 16 amp cables with multi-plugs on the end.

Take the white multi-plug and attach it to the 16 amp cable that powers the left (the side with the hole for the 16 amp cables), and place it by the left side.

![white multi-plug](images/multi-plug.webp)

There are four plugs coming from the rig that need to be plugged into the white multi-plug. These plugs are the power source for the vertical multi-plugs on the frames; during transit they are wrapped around the lights.

![plugs in transit](images/plug_around_light.webp)

Once all four are plugged in, take the black multi-plug (with three usable ports) to the other side, find the three plugs and plug them in.

That will power two-thirds of the rig. The last third is powered by the 32 amp cable and is turned on from the main breaker box: flip breakers 5 and 6 (PC) and the last of the rig should come alive.

## Frame 2
There is still a single frame left without power: Frame 2.

Go back to the plastic tub and find an ESPA power box that has NO blue tape on it. The power box should come with a power brick, but no kettle lead to power it. Take it over to Frame 2 — you should find a kettle lead wrapped around one of the nearby lights to power the ESPA box. At the base of the frame, all the camera DC power ports should be either hanging or tidied near the base; plug them all into the ESPA box and Frame 2 should come alive.

![frame 2 ESPA](images/frame_20_espa.webp)

## The Sonys
Head back to the plastic tub and retrieve the last two ESPA boxes with their power bricks. Place one on the outside of the left frame near the back of the trailer, and the other on the outside of the right frame near the back of the trailer.

The plastic tub should also contain several thick power cables with blue tape — these connect the Sonys to the ESPA boxes. Go to each Sony, find its power input port (it should have blue tape on it) and attach the cable. If the cable doesn't reach the ESPA box, use a second cable as an extension. There are enough cables for all the Sonys to be powered without being under tension.

![connecting to sony battery](images/sony_power.webp)

![sony kettle stored in transit](images/sny_kettle_around_light.webp)

# Framing
Now that everything is on, it's time to frame. Leave the mini-PCs and the main PC off for this.

First, find the long trigger cable that connects both sides of the trailer — it's usually wrapped up on a light. Make sure it goes under the floor mat.

## Linking the LED lights
Standing in the trailer door facing in, look behind the pillar on your right to find the LED light hardware. Make sure its two plugs are connected.

Then find the trigger cable with orange tape — this is what links the LED lights to the Sony trigger system. It plugs into a Sony start connector (blue tape) on frame 9; there should be a bit of orange tape on the connector's port.

Before plugging it in, thread the cable through one of the higher metal rings / eyelets so the cable is raised and not blocking the way.

Now check that the Canons are triggering fine. You should be able to press the shutter, hear the mirror open, then press again to hear the shutter open and take the photo. If you don't hear anything, or only one operation, there is a faulty connection.

All the star connectors that split the fire signal to the Canons are attached at a similar height on the middle horizontal bar across all frames. Go around and inspect each connection on each star (you will have to go once on the inside and once on the outside to access all the stars). If it's still not working, start isolating sections and debugging further.

Once all Canons are operational, you can finally begin framing.

::: note
This is the single longest task of the setup, so if anyone asks for an ETA, give yourself at least 3–4 hours from this point. Finishing early on a later ETA is better than finishing late on an optimistic one.
:::

::: note
Have someone stand in the centre as if they were being scanned. Only tell them to hold their hands in position when you frame the hand cameras — otherwise it's like being held in a stress position at a CIA black site.
:::

## What to frame where
There are different numbers of cameras on each frame, but there is a fairly simple rule set for what to frame where. Split the frame into two sections: top (above the middle bar) and bottom (below the middle bar). Ignore the Sonys for now.

## Top section
- All zoom lenses are to be fully zoomed out to see the entire marker board and the person.

- All 50 mm lenses are to be framed on the head. If the subject is short, put their head in the bottom half of the frame to make sure taller subjects aren't cut off, and vice versa.

## Bottom section
- The zoom lens directly below the bar is fully zoomed in and aimed at the subject's groin.

- The 50 mm next to the zoom lens points at the closest hand (left side points at the left hand, right side points at the right hand).

- The 50 mm below the hand camera points at the closest foot (make sure it's framed so it sees both the marker board and the foot).

- The 50 mm next to the foot is another hand camera.

- If there is a zoom lens at the bottom, it is fully zoomed out to see the full marker board and the person.

## Polarising filters
Most cameras will have a polarising filter, and it's vital that they are cross-polarised with the lights. To check this, point the camera at a light that has a filter (the one most directly opposite) and rotate the polarising filter — the light should swap between grey and black every 180 degrees. Leave it as black as possible.

Some cameras don't have polarising filters. This is because they are SPEC cameras, designed to show how specular light interacts with the subject.

## How to frame a Canon 50 mm camera
Follow these steps:

1.  Turn the mode to Av.

<!-- -->

1.  Flick the focus switch on the lens to its forward position to enable autofocus (note: there are a couple of cameras whose autofocus doesn't work and will have to be focused manually).

2.  Press the Live View button.

3.  Unscrew the mounted camera head's twist knob to allow it to move.

4.  Look at a polarised light and check it's cross-polarised.

5.  Look at the subject and half-press the shutter to autofocus.

6.  Lock the camera head in this position.

7.  Press the Live View button to end live view.

8.  Flick the focus switch on the lens backwards to disable autofocus.

9.  Turn the mode back to Manual.

## How to focus a Canon zoom lens
Follow the previous steps, except focus the camera both before and after polarising. Focusing the zoom lens can move the polarising filter, so we want to adjust the polarising filter once the focus is correct, then add a second focus afterwards to make sure you didn't nudge the focus wheel.

::: note
As you go round the cameras, if any won't turn on or have issues, this is the best time to solve them — try re-inserting the battery, or chase the power wires to look for disconnections. If you are under time pressure from production, you can leave faulty cameras and focus on getting a working rig first.
:::

# Checking Your Framing
Now you've framed, it's time to fully turn on the rig and check and refine.

Go around to all the mini-PCs and manually turn them on.

After the mini-PCs are on, look for the USB hubs that are either on the central bar or below it on the sides. Check that each one with a USB plugged in is turned on (some hubs are on when pushed in, some when pushed out); a blue light shows it's on, although you have to look carefully as the light can bleed to other ports. You will need to check both the inside and outside of the frame to find all the hubs.

## Bringing up Capture Grid
Turn on the main PC, log in, and open Cyber Controller from the pinned icon on the taskbar. It should display all the mini-PCs as connected; if not, restart the missing ones.

Open Capture Grid on the main PC, then press “Start all CG” to spawn Capture Grid nodes on the mini-PCs.

After about 30 seconds, the main PC's Capture Grid should display how many cameras are connected in the top right. Anything above 180 is very good; anything above 160 (with all PCs connected) is workable. Under 160 is reserved for catastrophic issues, like losing a power box.

## Checking camera settings
Head to the Cameras section in Capture Grid to work through the settings. The first two (mode and focus) can't be changed remotely — you have to go to the camera and fix it by hand — while the rest can be set directly in Capture Grid.

::: note
The camera name helps you find its position — look for Fxx_Cxx in the name, which tells you which frame it is on and where the camera is. Cameras are numbered as if you were reading left to right from inside the rig, so \_F08_C_03 is the 8th frame, third camera.
:::

1.  Sort by mode and find any cameras not on Manual. Use the Fxx_Cxx name to locate each one, then switch it to Manual by hand at the camera.

<!-- -->

1.  Sort by focus and find any on AF. These also need switching to MF by hand at the camera.

2.  Sort by ISO — any Canons not on 200, change to 200 directly in Capture Grid (click on their ISO and select 200).

3.  Sort by aperture — any SPEC camera (with ‘SPEC’ in the name) should be F22, and non-SPEC should be F11.

4.  Sort by shutter speed — Canons should be 1/25 and Sonys 1/30.

## Clearing live view
Now find any cameras that still have live view on. Press Prime, then in Capture Grid sort by MLU (mirror lock-up). Working cameras will display “on + up”; if a camera says just “on”, it's in live-view mode. Take a screenshot or phone picture of the faulty cameras, then press Fire to put all the mirrors back down. Head to each of those cameras and turn off live view.

## Checking the framing
Now it's time to check the framing. Get someone in the middle, turn the main lights off with the breaker, and press Fire-Single.

Once it's finished downloading, check through each image, looking at focus, framing and polarisation. If any are bad, make a note of their Fxx_Cxx ID and why they are bad. Go back, find and fix those cameras, delete the images, and fire another single fire. Keep repeating this process — firing and checking images — until all the pictures are correct.

::: note
If a couple are doing weird things but the settings seem correct, you can ignore them; it's usually power issues or genuinely faulty cameras.
:::

## Framing the Sonys
Finally, it's time to frame the Sonys. To look at a Sony's live view, press up twice on the front of it. It's dark, but you should be able to see enough with the main light on to check the framing; if not, change the shutter speed to make it brighter.

Usually only the \_02_01, \_02_02 and \_02_03 cameras will need adjusting. They should all see the head; \_02 and \_03 are wider, so try to get some of the torso in as well.

To focus the Sonys:

1.  Make sure someone is in the rig to be focused on. If they are small, tell them to hold a hand above their head and make themselves look bigger and taller, to give the Sonys a better chance.

<!-- -->

1.  Go to the left panel in Capture Grid, make sure Multiple is selected, then pick a Sony from the dropdown list of cameras.

2.  Make sure the main lights are on.

3.  Change the focus mode from MF to AF Continuous, then press Prime + Single Fire.

4.  Wait for the images to download, then switch the cameras back to MF. Keep the subject standing in the centre until they are back on MF.

5.  On the main Cameras page, sort by group ‘Sony’ (this makes them easier to see) and check they are all in manual focus.

6.  Delete the images, then go for another Prime + Single Fire.

7.  Inspect the focus on all the Sony images. If any are bad, repeat the last steps, or go to the out-of-focus one and use the manual focus wheel.

Now do a final full test fire of Prime + Fire and check the light pass is syncing.

# Final Check — Lights
The last thing to do is check that all the lights are working. In Cyber Controller, press ‘Cycle’ and all the lights should fire in sequence. You should be able to hear any gaps in the flashes; if you're unsure, cycle a few times and watch different sections to make sure every light is firing.

## If a light isn't firing
1.  Check that it's powered on. If it isn't, find where the disconnect is.

<!-- -->

1.  If it is on, press the \[Test\] button — it should flash. If it doesn't, the bulb might be blown; tell Theo and David, and run without it for now.

2.  If the test fire works, find and replug the trigger cable at the back of the light, then cycle again.

3.  If it still doesn't work, check the circuit board inside the PC cage for any loose connections.

Once all the lights are cycling cleanly, move on to the final disk-space check below.

# Disk Space Check
Once everything is working, the last check is disk space on the mini-PCs. On the main PC, go to Capture Grid and open the Network tab, then look for the disk space column — it shows how much space each mini-PC has left.

::: important
If a mini-PC's disk gets too full it starts delivering black images instead of erroring, so this is worth checking every time.
:::

If any mini-PC is under 250 GB, right-click its disk-space number and click Clean.

With the lights cycling cleanly and disk space clear, the trailer is set up and ready to scan.
